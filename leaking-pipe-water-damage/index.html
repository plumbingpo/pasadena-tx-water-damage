Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Pasadena

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. Water seeps out behind drywall, at the back of a sink cabinet or under the concrete until a stain, a soft floor or an unexplained water bill gives it away. By then the materials around the pipe have often been wet for weeks. We serve Pasadena homeowners by tracing the full wet area and drying every bit of it, including what is out of sight.

A plumber can stop the drip, though the water that soaked into the structure is still there afterward. Getting it out is the work we do. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. Mold often gets a head start during a long leak, which is why we look for it and treat the surfaces involved.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the WaterWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityWater around a switch, outlet or light fixture means the breaker for that part of the house should go off. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Check the Water MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
Coverage

Insurance and Leaking Pipe Water Damage

Insurance treats slow leaks differently from sudden ones. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Read your policy and talk to your agent. We document the source of the leak, moisture readings and photos so the adjuster has clear facts.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A compression fitting or supply line connection at a sink or toilet that has loosened or worn with use.
  • Hidden joints, soldered or crimped, that fail behind walls and above ceilings.
  • Water pressure set too high, which wears on every joint and valve until a slow weep begins.
  • Older copper pipe that corrodes from the inside until a pinhole opens in the wall of the line.
  • Water lines under a concrete slab that get rubbed through or stressed as the soil moves.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Swollen Cabinets

Particleboard cabinet bases under a sink swell and crumble once they absorb water. They do not return to shape, so long leaks often mean replacing the base.

Decayed Wall Framing

Wall studs and bottom plates that stay damp for weeks begin to soften and decay. Any piece too weak to carry load gets cut out and swapped for new lumber.

Mold Inside the Wall

A dark, damp wall cavity is where mold does best. It can spread across the hidden face of the drywall long before anything appears on the room side, which is common when a leak is slow.

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

Leaks over a ceiling or behind a wall surface show as bubbling paint and brown stains. Drywall that stays wet goes soft, sags and may need to be cut out.

How We Fix It

Our Leaking Pipe Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using a thermal camera and moisture meters, our crew checks cabinets, floors and walls and marks each wet spot, even those far from the pipe that water reached by running along the framing.

2

Make Targeted Openings

We make targeted openings to reach wet cavities, remove drywall, insulation or cabinet parts that cannot be saved, and bag any moldy material so spores are not spread through the house.

3

Structural Drying

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until the framing and subfloor reach normal moisture readings, usually 3 to 5 days. On every visit we take new readings and reposition equipment as things dry out.

4

Final Treatment and Records

Once the surfaces involved are treated with an antimicrobial, we hand over our photos and moisture logs. The records can help with a claim, and they confirm everything was dry before it was rebuilt.
